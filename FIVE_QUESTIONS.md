# The five questions (DRAFT built from the output in reports/analysis_output.txt; re-run the code, check each number, and rewrite in your own words)

**What is a row?** In the raw Excel sheet "Data", a row is one country-indicator pair with the years spread across columns: 75 rows were read, of which 70 are data (5 countries x 14 indicators) and 5 are footer or blank lines. After `load_and_reshape.py` a row is one country-year: 120 rows (5 x 24) and 14 indicator columns, with no duplicated country-year pairs. Confidence: high, because the counts reconcile exactly.

**What is the unit of the outcome?** The outcome is under-5 mortality, which the metadata defines as the probability per 1,000 that a newborn will die before age five if subject to that year's age-specific mortality rates. In our data it runs from 6.1 to 107.9 deaths per 1,000 live births (Sri Lanka 2023 to Pakistan 2000). It is a rate for a hypothetical newborn cohort, not a count of children who died that year. Confidence: high.

**What is the timeframe?** Annual values for 2000 to 2023, 24 years. The metadata gives a longer reference period for the series (for example 1960-2024 for under-5 mortality), so the 2000-2023 window is our choice from the download. Enrolment follows school years, and the metadata says the reference year is the year in which the school year ends. Confidence: high for the window, medium for exactly which year each estimate describes.

**Who is missing?** At country level, only five South Asian countries are included, so other countries in the region (for example Afghanistan, Bhutan and the Maldives; verify against the World Bank region list) are out by our selection, and countries outside South Asia are not represented. Inside the data, the gaps are in the education and birth-care indicators: skilled birth attendance is missing in 72 of 120 country-years (60%), adult female literacy in 62 (52%) and female secondary enrolment in 22 (18%). Sri Lanka has only 12 of 24 enrolment years (no values for 2000-2009 or for 2019-2020), Pakistan has 17 (missing 2000-2005 and 2020), Nepal 22 and Bangladesh 23, and India all 24. Enrolment is missing for 28% of country-years in 2000-2011 and 8% in 2012-2023, so the gaps are concentrated early. Rows with enrolment have a mean mortality of 50.3 against 44.9 for rows without it, so the missing rows are somewhat lower-mortality, not a random subset. Because every figure is a national average, subnational groups (rural and urban, provinces, income groups) and boys versus girls are also invisible. Confidence: high on the counts, low on why values are missing.

**What was the sampling frame?** There is no sample of our own; the frame is how each country figure was produced. Under-5 mortality is a modelled estimate: the metadata says the UN IGME fits a country-specific regression to all available vital registration, survey and census data and takes the trend line as the best estimate, and that complete vital registration is uncommon in developing countries. This fits what we see: the series is smooth, with no missing years, apart from the Sri Lanka spikes discussed in CLEANING.md. Female secondary enrolment comes from UNESCO's annual school survey divided by UN population estimates, so it is administrative data divided by a modelled denominator. Confidence: medium, because we have read the documentation but not the country-level inputs.
