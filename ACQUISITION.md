# Acquisition record

**Source and purpose.** The data comes from the World Bank's World Development Indicators (WDI), downloaded through DataBank as an Excel extract for Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nepal and Sri Lanka, covering 14 indicators for 2000-2023. We pulled it on 24 September 2026; the footer of the export says the database was last updated on 07/13/2026. The indicators are compiled by other agencies and republished by the World Bank, according to the metadata sheet: under-5 mortality by the UN Inter-agency Group for Child Mortality Estimation, female secondary enrolment by UNESCO's Institute for Statistics, fertility by the UN Population Division, health expenditure by WHO, and PPP GDP by the International Comparison Program. They were collected to monitor development goals (under-5 mortality is Sustainable Development Goal indicator 3.2.1) and to let countries be compared. Because they are national statistics built for comparison, they were not designed to answer our question about the role of female education.

**Acquisition method. A manual download from DataBank. We did not use the other methods because no suitable API call was needed, we did not scrape, and we did not run a survey of our own. The untouched file is in `data/raw/`, and `src/load_and_reshape.py` rebuilds the analysis from it. One caution: the workbook contains an extra sheet called "Sheet1", which is not part of the World Bank export. It is a manual reshape added by one of us, so the pipeline ignores it and reads only the "Data" sheet.

**Licence and privacy.** The metadata sheet lists the licence as CC BY-4.0, so reuse with attribution is allowed. The data are national aggregates with no individual-level records, so we see no privacy concern.

**Could a stranger re-pull this next month?** Mostly yes, but not with identical numbers. The metadata shows the series are revised, so a later download could shift values, add 2024 data and change which years are missing. The pipeline would break if DataBank changed the column names ("2000 [YR2000]") or the footer lines, because the loader keys on the "Series Code" column and the "[YR" year labels.
