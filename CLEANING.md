# Cleaning log (DRAFT: numbers come from reports/analysis_output.txt; check them, and rewrite the reasoning in your own words)

**1. Footer and blank rows (changed).** The raw "Data" sheet had 75 rows but only 70 are data (5 countries x 14 indicators); 5 are DataBank footer text ("Data from database: World Development Indicators", "Last Updated: 07/13/2026") or blank lines with no Series Code. `load_and_reshape.py` drops those 5 rows in code and leaves the Excel file untouched. I rejected deleting them by hand in Excel because the pipeline must re-run from the raw file.

**2. Missing values in enrolment (not changed).** Female secondary enrolment is missing in 22 of 120 country-years (Sri Lanka 12, Pakistan 7, Nepal 2, Bangladesh 1, India 0). I did not interpolate or forward-fill. Sri Lanka has no values for 2000-2009, so filling that gap would mean inventing a decade of data from the years after it. I rejected linear interpolation and country means for that reason. The cost is that the education analysis uses the 98 complete rows (saved as `data/processed/analysis_sample.csv`), and Sri Lanka contributes only 12 of them.

**3. Two columns left out of modelling (cells not changed).** Skilled birth attendance is missing in 72 of 120 country-years (60%) and adult female literacy in 62 (52%). Sri Lanka has only 4 years of birth attendance and Nepal 6 years of literacy, so imputing them would mean most of the column was made up. I kept the columns in the panel file but left them out of the models; I rejected imputing them. A possible next step is to re-add them with a model that handles missing values explicitly.

**4. Sri Lanka mortality spikes (not changed).** Sri Lanka under-5 mortality jumps from 15.2 in 2003 to 29.0 in 2004, then falls to 14.0 in 2005, and rises from 12.0 in 2008 to 21.5 in 2009 before returning to 11.2 in 2010. These are the largest single-year changes in the mortality series (15.0 points in 2005). They look like one-year shocks that I suspect reflect the 2004 tsunami and the end of the civil war in 2009, but I have not confirmed that in the UN IGME documentation, so check it before you claim it. I kept them because deleting real events would hide the history. They do not affect the education analysis because the enrolment sample starts in 2010 for Sri Lanka, but they are in the 120-row prediction data. I rejected winsorising or removing them.

**5. Pakistan unemployment break (flagged, left in).** Pakistan unemployment is 0.58 to 0.65 until 2011, then 0.80 in 2011 and 3.67 in 2012, a rise of 361% in one year, which is the biggest relative jump in any indicator. A change that sudden looks like a methodology or source change in the modelled ILO estimate, but I have not confirmed it. I left it in and tested the effect: dropping unemployment changes the leave-one-country-out ridge model from MAE 15.5 (R2 0.55) to MAE 14.3 (R2 0.60) and leaves the time-split results essentially unchanged, so the model is not driven by it. I rejected deleting Pakistan's pre-2012 values.

**6. Log of GDP per capita (new column).** GDP per capita (PPP, constant 2021 international dollars) has skew 1.45; `log_gdp` has skew 0.58, which makes the relationship with mortality closer to linear and stops the richest country dominating. All 120 rows get the new column and the original is kept. I rejected standardising instead because the interpretation per log-point is easier to explain.

**Six-things check.** Missing: found, as described above (checked by counting empty cells per indicator; there were 0 cells containing ".."). Duplicates: not found, 0 duplicated country-year pairs after reshaping. Wrong types: not found, every indicator column is numeric after loading. Wrong units: not found, all eight percentage-type columns lie within 0-100 (0 violations) and mortality ranges 6.1-107.9; I did not find a value above 100 in the gross enrolment ratio, although it can exceed 100 by definition. Encoding: not found, no stray whitespace or non-ASCII characters in the 5 country names, and no regional aggregates (the only entities are the five countries). Drift: found, in Pakistan unemployment (2012) and Sri Lanka mortality (2004, 2009), checked by computing the largest single-year change per series; the metadata also says PPP GDP is extrapolated from 2011 and 2021 benchmarks, so GDP is less reliable away from those years.
